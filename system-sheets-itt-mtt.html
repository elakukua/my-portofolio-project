Google Sheets · ITT · MTT

The working layer where project figures are reconciled before they become reports.

The Indicator Tracking Table holds what was planned. The Monitoring Tracking Table holds what was recorded. Most reporting problems are a disagreement between the two that nobody looked for.

StackGoogle Sheets
RunsEvery reporting period
CoversCleaning, analysis, visualisation
FeedsDonor and management reporting
STEP 01
↓
Structure both tables to the same spine

Reconciliation is only possible if both tables can be joined on the same keys. That is a design decision, not a later fix.

What is needed
  • A shared indicator code carried identically in both tables
  • Consistent period definitions — the same fiscal or project period boundaries on both sides
  • The same disaggregation categories, in the same order, with the same labels
  • One owner per table, so changes are deliberate rather than accumulated
Why it matters
  • Two tables that cannot be joined are two opinions, and the reconciliation becomes manual comparison of hundreds of cells
STEP 02
↓
Clean and validate

Before comparison, each table has to be internally sound.

Process
  • Data validation on entry: dropdowns for indicator codes, periods and categories, so typing cannot introduce a new category
  • Check totals against their own disaggregation — the parts should reconstruct the whole
  • Flag blank achievements against periods that have closed, rather than reading blank as zero
  • Remove error-suppressing formulas so a broken calculation shows as broken
Principle
  • A wrapper that hides an error does not fix it; it defers it to whoever reads the report
STEP 03
↓
Reconcile in both directions

Checking that every ITT line has an MTT counterpart is only half the work. The other half finds what was recorded but never planned for.

Process
  • Direction one: for every indicator and period in the ITT, is there a corresponding achievement in the MTT?
  • Direction two: for every achievement recorded in the MTT, does it map to a planned indicator and period in the ITT?
  • Classify each discrepancy: missing period, transcription error, definitional difference, or genuine change
  • Log the resolution and who decided it — the log answers the auditor's question before it is asked
What one-directional checks miss
  • Achievements recorded against periods nobody planned
  • Indicators being reported that were dropped from the design
  • Copy-paste errors that duplicate a previous period's figures into a new one
STEP 04
Visualise for the people who have to act

The tracking tables are a working layer; the views built on them are what programme staff actually read.

Process
  • Progress against target by indicator and period, with the gap stated rather than implied
  • A discrepancy view listing unresolved reconciliation items, so they are visible instead of remembered
  • Views built for the audience: field teams need the indicator level, management needs the outcome level
Result
  • Discrepancies surface here first, weeks before a donor report would have exposed them
Why this is the highest-yield check in MEAL
  • It is where the money and the evidence meet. Donor reports are built from these tables; an error here becomes an error in a signed document.
  • It catches problems no dashboard would. A dashboard faithfully displays a missing period as a low number, not as a missing period.
  • It creates the decision log. Every correction carries a reason, which is what turns a corrected figure into a defensible one.
