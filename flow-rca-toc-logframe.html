Programme Design · RCA · ToC · Logframe

What a programme measures is decided before anyone collects anything.

Child well-being, most vulnerable children, GESI, safeguarding and community perspectives enter at the analysis stage — not as a later addition. Outputs and outcomes are determined in the Theory of Change, not read mechanically off the problem tree.

The design chain — each output feeds the next

02Root Cause Analysis
03Objective Analysis

01

Situation Analysis

  • Analyse context, stakeholders, evidence and previous learning.
  • Apply child well-being, MVC, GESI, safeguarding and community perspectives.
  • Output: prioritised problem and evidence statement.
02

Root Cause Analysis

  • Develop a participatory problem tree.
  • Distinguish immediate, underlying and root causes.
  • Identify the causes the programme can influence.
  • Output: evidence-based causal analysis.
03

Objective Analysis

  • Restate priority problems as desired positive changes.
  • Assess feasibility, influence, partners and scope.
  • Validate the means-to-ends logic.
  • Output: prioritised change objectives.
04

Theory of Change

  • Define impact and work backwards through outcomes and outputs.
  • Map causal pathways, actors, assumptions, risks and external factors.
  • Attach evidence and identify World Vision's contribution.
  • Output: ToC diagram and narrative.
05

Logframe

  • Translate the ToC into clear results statements.
  • Define indicators, baselines, targets, disaggregation, MoV and assumptions.
  • Use standard indicators where relevant and justify custom indicators.
  • Output: validated results framework or logframe.
06

MEAL Plan & Tools

  • Develop indicator reference sheets, ITT, monitoring and data-management plans.
  • Prepare data-collection tools, evaluation plan, accountability mechanism and learning agenda.
  • Assign frequency, responsibilities, data quality checks and reporting use.
  • Output: operational MEAL package.
Design principle

Outputs and outcomes are decided in the Theory of Change, not inherited automatically from the problem tree.

The objective analysis states what positive change is wanted. The Theory of Change then determines the pathway, the contribution, the assumptions and what is realistically achievable.
